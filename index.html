Estimating Your Probability of Dismissal

Suppose your law school has 150 students, and the lowest-performing 15 (10%) are dismissed at the end of the year. Without further information about your standing, your probability of dismissal is

P(Dismissal)=15150=10%

Now suppose you know a sample of classmates and feel confident you'll outperform some of them. Bayes' theorem lets us estimate your dismissal probability from the size of your sample, the number of favorable judgments you make, and their reliability.

The mathematical model

Let B be the number of classmates you actually outperform, m the number you assess, and K the number you judge you will outperform. Initially, every rank is equally likely, so B is uniformly distributed from 0 to 149. Dismissal means B ≤ 14.

Among the m sampled classmates, let J be the number you truly outperform. For each classmate, you make a confident prediction in either direction with probability q = 0.5. When you make a prediction, it is correct with probability p. Comparisons without a confident prediction are left unresolved.

J|B=b∼Hypergeom(149,b,m)
K|J=j∼Bin(j,qp)+Bin(m−j,q(1−p))

Writing Lb = P(K = k | B = b, m, p, q), Bayes' theorem gives

P(D|m,k,p,q)= ∑b=014Lb ∑b=0149Lb

Assumptions and limitations

  • Prior rankings: Before considering your comparisons, each possible class rank is equally likely.
  • Representative sample: The classmates you know are modeled as a random sample of the other 149 students.
  • Judgments: For each classmate, you make a confident prediction about who will do better with probability q = 0.5, independently of the true ranking. Each prediction is correct with probability p, regardless of its direction. Otherwise, you remain uncertain.
  • Independence: Prediction and uncertainty outcomes are independent across classmates, conditional on their true rankings.
  • Fixed threshold: Exactly 15 of 150 students are dismissed, with no ties.

These are illustrative model estimates, not a calibrated personal forecast. The stated accuracy p should reflect how reliably your predictions correspond to final rankings, rather than confidence alone.
